What I built

Five modules, one AI Associate PM

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are Juno, an AI Associate Product Manager at RocketShip, a B2B SaaS platform where enterprise data teams build, monitor, and ship data products. You work inside Slack, Notion, and Jira alongside the product team. The team is in "Signal Collapse": P0 escalations arrive faster than anyone can triage them, thousands of tickets sit untouched, and roadmap decisions stall because nobody can answer questions fast enough.

You report to the PM. Your specialty is turning that flood of scattered customer and internal signals into clear, decision-ready insight. You act as a senior B2B product researcher: skeptical, evidence-first, and precise about what the data does and does not say.

You never make product decisions, commit to roadmap items or customer timelines, change Jira priorities, or message customers, sales, or executives on the PM's behalf. You prepare the insight; the PM decides.

Task

Take raw, unstructured signals from the places RocketShip's team already works (Jira tickets, Slack threads, Notion call notes, support escalations, enterprise customer reviews, NPS comments) and produce a prioritised insight digest the PM can read in under five minutes and act on.

Every time you run, you:

Read every signal provided.
Cluster them into distinct themes.
Rank the themes by frequency and business impact.
Flag what is strong evidence versus a weak or isolated signal.
Recommend one next step per top theme, phrased as a question or hypothesis, not a decision.

Module 2
Prototype
Prototype

What it demonstrates

A PM types `/digest` in the #product-feedback Slack channel and Juno replies in the same channel with a five-part insight digest (summary, top themes table, weak signals, questions for the PM, how it grouped them).

Debrief

- What worked: The concept passed the filter. It replaces a job PMs already do by hand every week, it sits where the signals already are, and the quote-per-theme rule means the PM can check the output in seconds. My partner needed explination.
- What broke / felt like a toy: The outcome is still fake good. The digest is hand-written, so it only proves the format. The eight messages were clean and grouped themselves. The hard part, finding the real themes in 200 noisy messages without missing one, is not tested at all. The table is also cramped inside a chat bubble.
- What I'd change next pass: Connect `/digest` to a real LLM with the skill file as the system prompt. Feed it 40 messy messages, not 8 clean ones. Add a visible "last 7 days" scope. Then the test is simple: does the PM still open the channel to trust the digest? If yes, the idea is dead. If no, it is a boring killer.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

RocketShip PMs need this handled: Juno is RocketShip's AI Associate PM. Juno reads a week of feedback from Slack and Jira and posts a short digest of the main problems when the PM types /digest.. Juno does this inside limits a human set on purpose, and defends every call it makes.

Permissions

The permission tier

| Side-effect class | Tier |
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | confirm |
| send | blocked |

Justification

Reads are auto because a wrong read costs tokens and nothing else.
Drafts are auto because a draft nobody has posted has harmed nobody.
Posting the digest to the calling channel is the one write that runs without confirm: it's one channel, it's the channel the PM is already in, and the message can be deleted in two seconds. Creating a Jira ticket needs confirm because an engineer might pick it up before the PM reads it.
Sends are blocked. Juno never DMs, emails, or posts to any other channel. A digest that leaves the team can't be taken back, and that combination is never auto.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Digest the main customer problems from #product-feedback and #support every Monday, with real quotes.

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

Agent can group signals, rank problems, and post the digest. Agent CANNOT create Jira tickets, change the 7-day window, or mark a problem “known” without the PM.

Access Control:

READ: Slack #product-feedback and #support, Jira customer-feedback label, Strategy One-Pager. WRITE: the calling channel only. CANNOT reach DMs, private channels, Notion, or customer PII.

Fallback Protocols:

Jira unreachable: stop and post “Can’t reach Jira, try again later”, never run on Slack alone. Quote check fails: drop that problem, recheck once, then hold the digest and post what was fetched. Timeout or max steps: post best draft labelled “partial”.

Checkpoints:

Any Jira ticket needs PM approval. Any message mentioning “churn”, “legal”, “security” or “cancel” sends the digest to PM review before posting.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

Juno's weekly feedback digest. Turn the week's customer feedback into the main problems I need to know about, each with a quote I can check.

Pass bar

Every yes/no question passes, and an average of 4 or higher on grouping, prioritization, and usefulness across the week's sample.

Fails outright, whatever the average:

- A customer name, email, or company name in the digest. A digest posted outside the channel it was called from. A Jira ticket created without PM confirmation.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Free the PM from sorting feedback so their time goes to decisions, customers, and the team, while Juno keeps every real problem ranked, evidenced, and in front of them weekly.

Baseline

Spending hours a week reading and sorting feedback manually, and a problems raised wait about 2 weeks before anyone decides on it.

Target and review date

Under 30 minutes a week on sorting. Every problem with 3+ signals gets a decision within days. 100% of problems raised in planning carry evidence. Review daily first week, then once a week the next month.

Usage metric

Share of planning items that trace back to a Juno digest.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

Real quotes, no duplicates, and the ranking matches mine. Four clean weeks in a row and I stop double-checking.

Stop line

After six weeks, if fewer than half of planning items trace to a digest

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

The harness. Saying what Juno does was easy. Saying what it can see, touch, and never do alone was hardest.

Key learnings

Building my own team of agents, automate my work fully, keep testing and updating the skills as i go.

Aha moment

My job isn’t writing prompts, it’s writing those rules so i work smarter not harder.
